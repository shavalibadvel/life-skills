**How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A, and P.**

***answer:***
        
 Shrink the new habit to a version that takes 30 seconds or less, such as doing one push-up, flossing one tooth, or taking one mindful breath,. Making the task effortless means you need almost no motivation to cross the action line and get it done.

 ## Question 3
 
**Why is it important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of a habit? (This is the most important concept in today's topic. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit).**

***answer:***

Shine is that genuine feeling of pride and accomplishment you give yourself immediately after finishing a tiny habit like saying "I'm awesome!" or doing a small fist task. Celebrating right away gives the habit into your brain—even if the action was tiny—creating success momentum that makes you want to repeat it and naturally grow the habit over time.

## Question 5

**What is the book's perspective about Identity?**

***answer:***

The book argues that lasting change starts with identity rather than outcomes. Instead of focusing on what you want to achieve like "I want to read a book" or "I want to lose weight", you focus on who you want to become "I am a reader" or "I am a healthy person". Every small habit you repeat acts as a vote for that new identity until you genuinely believe it about yourself.


## Question 6

**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?**

***answer:***

To make a good habit easier, you have to reduce friction in your environment so there are fewer steps between you and the good behavior. For example, keeping a book right on top of your pillow so you see it before bed, or keeping a musical instrument next to your desk. You also optimize for the starting line using the two-minute rule—focusing on making the first two minutes effortless so getting started feels automatic.

## Question 7

**Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?**

***answer:***

To break a bad habit, you do the exact opposite: increase friction and put more steps between yourself and the behavior. For instance, hiding social media apps inside folders several swipes away, logging out of accounts so you have to re-enter passwords, or leaving your phone in another room while working. When a bad habit takes extra effort, you stop doing it mindlessly.


## Question 8

**Pick one habit that you would like to do more of? What are the steps that you can take to make the cue obvious, the habit more attractive or easy, and the response satisfying?**

***answer:***

*Habit:* Solving at least one coding or DSA problem every day.

*Make it obvious:* Keep my coding platform tab pinned and open on my laptop screen every evening after my work ends, and anchor it to my evening routine

*Make it attractive:*  Pair my practice session with a cup of coffee and chill lo-fi music so I look forward to sitting down for it.

*Make it easy:* Commit to just reading the problem and writing the basic function outline in under 2 minutes so there's zero pressure to solve a hard problem right away.

*Make it satisfying:* Mark a green checkmark on a daily streak tracker right after submitting a solution and take a quick 5-minute break to celebrate completing it.

## Question 9

**Pick one habit that you would like to eliminate or do less of? What are the steps that you can take to make the cue invisible, the process unattractive or hard, and the response unsatisfying?**

***answer:***

*Habit:* Mindlessly scrolling on my phone late at night before sleep.

*Make the cue invisible:* Put my phone on Do Not Disturb and leave it charging across the room instead of next to my pillow.

*Make it unattractive:* Remind myself how i was and unfocused I feel the next morning whenever I stay up scrolling.

*Make it hard:* Turn on grayscale mode at night so the screen looks dull, and remove distracting apps from my home screen so they take multiple taps to open.

*Make it unsatisfying:* Set strict app time limits that lock the screen after 15 minutes, breaking the endless scroll loop immediately.
